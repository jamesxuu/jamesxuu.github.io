About Me

I am a PhD student in Quantitative Bioscience in the School of Physics at Georgia Tech, advised by Prof. Daniel I. Goldman. I am also pursuing an MS in Robotics/Computer Science.

My research uses physical experiments, robot design, and modeling to understand how multi-legged systems sense, adapt, and move through complex environments. I am especially interested in tactile sensing, terrain interaction, and locomotion across land and water.

Before Georgia Tech, I completed an MS in Biomedical Engineering at Columbia University and a BEng in Electrical Engineering at University College London.

Research Interests

01

Tactile sensing for multi-legged robots

I develop compliant antennae that provide tactile feedback during locomotion over rugged terrain, connecting physical sensing directly to robot control.

First-author work accepted to IROS 2026.
02

Multi-terrain and surface-swimming robots

I design and test undulatory robots with morphable, directionally compliant legs for terrestrial and water-surface locomotion, using robophysical experiments and resistive force theory.

Work in Physical Review Applied; presented at APS and SICB.
03

Robust locomotion in complex terrain

I study geometric, probabilistic, and contact-based gait control for multi-legged elongated robots moving across noisy and rugged landscapes.

Work in IEEE Transactions on Robotics and ICRA.
